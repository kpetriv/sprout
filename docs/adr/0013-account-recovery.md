# ADR-0013: Account recovery for a parent who lost every phone

- **Status:** Accepted (design; coordinator, 2026-11-13). Implementation: branch `fix/account-recovery`, release 1.1.1.
- **Date:** 2026-11-13
- **Related:** [ADR-0004](0004-parent-authorisation-and-device-identity.md) (parent PIN, device keys), [ADR-0007](0007-identity-linking-and-device-registry.md) (device registry, approval), THREAT_MODEL T7, T8, T33, T41; RUNBOOK §2 (`recover-household`).
- **Decisions:** D-REL-42 onwards in `docs/DECISIONS-PENDING.md`.

## Context

A parent phone that lost its app data (Clear storage, an uninstall, a factory reset, a lost phone) has lost its device key: the Keystore deletes it with the app. Signing in again with the same Google account registers a new device, and a new parent device stays PENDING until an ACTIVE parent device of the account approves it (T8). A household has one parent account, and most have one parent phone, so nothing can approve: the parent sits on "Waiting for approval" for ever. Until now the only way out was the operator's `recover-household` script (RUNBOOK §2, accepted risk "a household with a single parent device cannot recover from losing it in the app"). Found on the first real-device test of 1.1.0 (owner, 2026-11-13): "there has to be a way to restore access to the same account from a different device or the same device after a reinstall".

What recovery must not do:

- **Hand the parent area to whoever holds the parent's unlocked phone.** Google sign-in through Credential Manager is one tap on a phone that has the account; it asks for no password. If the Google account alone recovered the household, a kid holding the parent's phone could clear Sprout's storage, sign in, recover and choose a new PIN. The parent PIN is the kid-versus-parent boundary of this app (ADR-0004), so recovery needs something the kid does not have.
- **Hand the household to whoever took over the Google account**, at once and without the parent noticing (T41).

## Decision

Two ways to recover, both self-service, both on the "Waiting for approval" screen of the new (PENDING) phone:

1. **With the parent PIN, at once.** The server keeps a check value of the parent PIN and compares it (ten wrong attempts close this way, §2).
2. **Without the PIN, after a wait of 7 days** (the operator's `RECOVERY_WAITING_DAYS`, T41) that starts when the PENDING phone asks for it, during which a working phone of the account shows the waiting phone with a warning and can remove it.

Either way the PENDING phone becomes ACTIVE and **every other parent device of the account is revoked** exactly as `revokeDevice` revokes it (`revokedAtLamport`, parent keys stamped), every session of the account is revoked (the recovering phone signs in once more), and a `recoveries` record is written. Approval from a working phone stays as it is and is still the first thing the screen offers.

### 1. The recovery PIN check value

- The **client** derives `verifier = KDF(utf8(pin), salt)`, 32 bytes, where `salt` = 16 random bytes chosen by the client when the check value is set, and the KDF is Argon2id with the parameters of `Argon2idPinHasher` (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 with the parameters of `Pbkdf2PinHasher` where Argon2id is unavailable, and on the JVM in tests). The KDF input is domain-separated: the Argon2id/PBKDF2 password is `"sprout/recovery-pin/v1" || 0x00 || hid || 0x00 || utf8(pin)`. No device pepper: the value must be derivable on a phone that has nothing but the PIN. The record carries a `kdf` descriptor string (algorithm and parameters) so a recovering phone derives with the parameters the value was set with; a phone that cannot compute the recorded KDF says so and offers the wait.
- The **server** stores, in `recoveryPins/{uid}` (top level, no client access under the rules): `{ hid, salt, kdf, mac, failedAttempts, setAt, setByDeviceId }` where `mac = HMAC-SHA256(k, hid || 0x00 || uid || 0x00 || verifier)` and `k = HKDF-SHA256(RECOVERY_PIN_HMAC_KEY, info = "sprout/recovery-pin/v1")`, a secret of its own (audit L2: a leaked link-code key must not make PINs searchable, and rotating one must not disturb the other; production refuses to start the PIN callables without it, the emulator uses a public dev key). The key lives only in Vercel's sensitive variables, so a copy of the database alone does not allow guessing PINs offline; the operator (server plus database) could guess a 6-digit PIN, and is already trusted for device admission (D-M4-16). The verifier itself is never stored or logged. The row also carries `keyId` (the first 16 bytes of `HMAC(k, "sprout/recovery-pin/key-id")`, hex): after a rotation of `RECOVERY_PIN_HMAC_KEY` a value made under the old key counts as absent (`pin: null`, `pin-unavailable`) and uses up no attempt. The phone cannot see a rotation, so its "registered" mark is a time and the app registers again at a correct PIN entry once the mark is older than 30 days.
- `setRecoveryPin({ kdf, newPin, salt, verifier, callerDeviceId, signature })`: PARENT caller, device proof by an ACTIVE parent device over `callablePayload('setRecoveryPin', hid, idTokenHash, { kdf, newPin, salt, verifier })`. Replaces the row. `newPin: true` (the PIN was just chosen or changed) starts `failedAttempts` at 0; `newPin: false` (the same PIN registered again: first registration after the update, the 30-day refresh) keeps the count of an existing row of the same household, so a refresh never hands a guesser new attempts and a closed PIN path stays closed until the PIN is changed or a recovery by waiting removes the row. Read, check and write happen in one transaction with the member row, so a household being deleted cannot get its row back. Answers `{ set: true, stored, attemptsLeft }`: `stored` is false only when the call left another phone's value in place (next bullet); the phone keeps `attemptsLeft` so Settings can say that wrong PINs were tried.
- **One value per account, several phones.** The PIN is local to each phone, and two phones of the same parent account can have different PINs, while the server keeps one check value per account. The value is that of **the PIN set or changed most recently on any of the account's phones**: a `newPin: true` call always replaces the row; a `newPin: false` call (first registration after the update, the 30-day refresh, the first PIN entry after a PIN restore) leaves an existing row alone when that row was made under the current key, for the same household, by another device that is still ACTIVE, and answers `{ set: true, stored: false, attemptsLeft }` from the row it kept. The phone then shows "this account restores with the PIN you set or changed last, on your other phone" instead of "ready", and does not count itself registered (app audit M2); it asks again at a correct PIN entry once that answer is a day old, so a removed phone's PIN stops restoring within a day of the next unlock rather than 30 (review N3). Otherwise the refreshes of two phones would flip the value every month and nobody could say which PIN restores. The copy says "the parent PIN you set or changed last". A row whose device is REVOKED (after a restore, or a removed phone) is replaced by the next registration. A phone never carries a local PIN into a restore: registering as a PENDING second parent device clears any parent PIN and lockout already on the phone (only debug and benchmark builds can have one, from the local trial), so a PIN restore always adopts the PIN the server accepted and every other admission chooses its PIN after admission (app audit L1).
- The app calls it whenever it holds the PIN in memory and the check value on the server is not known to match it: after the PIN is set (onboarding), after it is changed (Settings), and after a correct PIN entry while a local flag says "not registered" (first unlock after the update for households made before 1.1.1, or an earlier upload that failed). The flag is cleared by every PIN change and set by a successful call that answered `stored: true`. A PIN change also sets a second, non-secret mark, "new PIN not uploaded yet", cleared by the first successful upload after it; while it is set every upload says `newPin: true`, so a change whose upload failed (offline, process death) still replaces the old value and its count at the next correct PIN entry (app audit M2). "Changing" the PIN to the PIN it already is sends `newPin: false` and sets no mark, so re-typing the same PIN does not hand a guesser ten new attempts (app audit L3). Nothing derived from the PIN is stored on the device for a later upload. Settings → Security shows "Account recovery: ready" or "Enter your PIN to finish setting up account recovery" (the row asks for the PIN and uploads).
- The row is deleted with the household (`deleteHouseholdData`, per uid) and by a recovery without the PIN.

### 2. The callables of a PENDING phone

Both take the device proof of the **PENDING** device itself: `deviceId` and `signature` over `callablePayload(<callable>, hid, idTokenHash, <args>)` with that device's registered key (a new verification helper next to `verifyDeviceProof`: the row must exist, be the caller's, role PARENT, status PENDING).

- `recoveryStatus({ deviceId, signature })` → `{ pin: { salt, kdf } | null, attemptsLeft, waitEndsAt }`. `pin` is null when no check value is stored or the attempts are used up. `waitEndsAt` (epoch milliseconds) is null until the phone has asked for the wait, then the device row's `recoveryRequestedAt` + 7 days. Reading the status never starts the wait.
- `recoverParentDevice({ method: 'PIN' | 'WAIT', verifier?, deviceId, signature })` → `{ deviceId, status: 'ACTIVE', revokedDevices, method, attemptsLeft, signInAgain: true }` (`revokedDevices` is a count). `verifier` is required with `PIN` and refused with `WAIT`.
  - Requires a session signed in within the last 5 minutes (`RECENT_SIGN_IN_MS`, as `deleteHousehold`; `failed-precondition` otherwise, and the app signs in again and retries once).
  - Refused (`failed-precondition`) when another parent account of the household has an ACTIVE device (cannot happen in v1; kept from the operator script).
  - **`PIN`:** in one transaction, read the row; no row or `failedAttempts >= 10` → `failed-precondition` ("PIN recovery is not available"); MAC mismatch (constant time) → increment `failedAttempts`, commit, answer `permission-denied` with `details: { attemptsLeft }`; match → recover, keep the row and its `failedAttempts` (wrong guesses add up for as long as the PIN stays the same, also across recoveries; audit M1). Ten wrong PINs in total close PIN recovery for the account until a working phone registers a **new** PIN (`newPin: true`) or a recovery by waiting removes the row: at most 10 guesses in 1,000,000 per PIN.
  - **`WAIT`:** the first call writes `recoveryRequestedAt` (server time, written once, never moved) on the PENDING device row and answers `failed-precondition` with `details: { reason: "wait", waitEndsAt }`; so does every call before `recoveryRequestedAt` + 7 days. After that it recovers and deletes the `recoveryPins` row (the PIN is forgotten; the new one is registered when it is set). The wait does not start at registration (audit M2): a phone that was left PENDING long ago, or a row that was already stale when this shipped, would otherwise be able to take over at once. `registerParentDevice` for the same pending key does not touch the field.
  - **Recover** (one transaction, the logic of the operator's `recover-household` moved into `server/src` and shared with the script): the device → ACTIVE with `approvedBy: 'recovery'`, `recoveredAt`, `recoveryMethod`; every other non-revoked PARENT device of the uid → `planRevocation`/`applyRevocation`; `recoveries/{autoId}` = `{ hidHash, at, deviceId, revokedDevices, method }` (no uid, no names; `ticket: null`).
  - **Sessions:** as `revokeDevice`: inside the transaction `members/{uid}.tokensValidAfter` is raised to the recovering token's `auth_time` (never lowered), and after it `revokeParentSessions` revokes the account's refresh tokens and moves `tokensValidAfter` to Auth's value; the answer carries `signInAgain: true` and the recovering phone signs in once more before its first sync. The first draft kept the recovering session and skipped `revokeRefreshTokens`; the audit (H1) showed that reads are not tied to `tokensValidAfter` and that the lost phone's wipe starts only when its token refresh is refused, so a lost phone would have kept reading. When the Auth call fails the device row keeps `sessionsRevocationPending: true`, the call answers `internal`, and a repeated `recoverParentDevice` from the now ACTIVE device finishes the revocation and answers as the first call would have (the operator script's "finish an unfinished session revocation" step sees the same mark).
  - Rate limits: per IP before anything is read, then per device once its proof has verified. Not per uid: whoever holds the Google account could otherwise keep the account's bucket full and lock the real parent out of restoring or of registering the check value.
  - Logs: counts and ids only (`device.recovered` with hid, deviceId, method, revoked count; a wrong PIN as `device.recoveryRefused` with attemptsLeft). Never the verifier, salt or MAC.

### 3. The app

- **Waiting for approval** keeps the fingerprint and the "approve on your other phone" text, then: "Don't have your other phone?" with
  - **Restore with your Sprout PIN** (when `recoveryStatus.pin` is not null): a PIN entry screen (the lock feature's PIN pad, `FLAG_SECURE`), "Enter the parent PIN you used before". Wrong: "That PIN doesn't match. N tries left." No tries left or no check value: the entry is replaced by the wait text.
  - **Forgot your PIN?** / no check value: "You can restore this account without the PIN after a 7-day wait. If another phone still has Sprout, it will show this phone and can approve or remove it." with a **Start the 7-day wait** button (the first `WAIT` call). Then: "You can restore this account on <date>." On and after that date: a "Restore this account" button.
  - Before either restore: one confirmation, "Restore on this phone? Every other phone signed in to this parent account is removed from the household. Earlier chat messages can't be read on this phone; everything else comes back." Restore / Cancel.
- After a restore the phone signs in with Google once more (`signInAgain`; a PIN restore first stores the local PIN hash, so a cancelled sign-in loses nothing: the screen says "Sign in again to finish" and a restart restores the now ACTIVE phone the usual way), then first sync, then the lock step. A restore **with the PIN keeps that PIN** (the lock step skips "choose a PIN" and goes to the fingerprint/signing step; the local PIN hash is created from the PIN just entered). The phone adopts the PIN only when the answer says `method: "PIN"`, and it does not count itself registered: the next correct PIN entry registers with `newPin: false`, which the server stores (the device that set the old value is REVOKED by then) with the count kept. So even when the answer came from the "finish the revocation" path, which does not compare a PIN, the phone's PIN and the server's value end up the same (app audit N1). A restore **by waiting** runs the normal "choose a PIN" step, which registers the new check value.
- A working phone's "a parent's phone wants to join" card says that a phone left waiting can ask to take over the account after 7 days and should be removed if it is not the parent's. Showing the date there needs the registry row's new field in the local model and is a follow-up (D-REL-49).
- Settings → Security shows how many wrong PINs were tried in a restore (from `attemptsLeft`) and says to change the PIN; at 0 it says the PIN way is closed until the PIN is changed.
- Fingerprint enrolment, the parent key (`registerParentKey`), the chat key and the household policy then follow the paths a newly approved second phone already takes (D-REL-11, D-REL-14). The phone that was revoked, if it still exists, learns it from `deviceStatus` and removes its copy as for any removal.
- `feature/onboarding` talks to ports (`OnboardingPorts.kt`); the callables live in `core/sync-firebase` behind the existing backend interface with a fake in `core/sync` testFixtures. The KDF lives in `core/crypto` (`RecoveryPinVerifier`). `feature/lock` reports "the PIN is known" through a port the app implements.

### 4. Wire format (both sides implement exactly this)

| Thing | Value |
|---|---|
| Signing domains (`SIGNED_CALLABLES`) | `setRecoveryPin`: `sprout/set-recovery-pin/v2`; `recoveryStatus`: `sprout/recovery-status/v2`; `recoverParentDevice`: `sprout/recover-parent-device/v2` |
| Signed bytes | `callablePayload(callable, hid, sha256(raw ID token), args)` as for every signed callable; ECDSA P-256/SHA-256 DER signature, standard base64 |
| Signed `args` | `setRecoveryPin`: `{ kdf, newPin, salt, verifier }` (`newPin` a JSON boolean); `recoveryStatus`: `{}`; `recoverParentDevice`: `{ method: "PIN", verifier }` or `{ method: "WAIT" }` |
| Proof fields (not signed) | `setRecoveryPin`: `callerDeviceId`, `signature` (an ACTIVE parent device, `verifyDeviceProof`); the other two: `deviceId`, `signature` (the PENDING device itself) |
| `salt` | 16 bytes, standard base64 |
| `verifier` | 32 bytes, standard base64 |
| `kdf` | `argon2id$v=19$m=65536,t=3,p=1` (devices) or `pbkdf2-sha256$i=600000` (fallback, JVM tests). The server stores and returns it and checks only the shape: `^(argon2id\$v=19\$m=\d{1,7},t=\d{1,2},p=\d)$` or `^pbkdf2-sha256\$i=\d{1,8}$`. A recovering client refuses parameters above its own bounds (Argon2id: m ≤ 262144 KiB, t ≤ 10, p ≤ 4; PBKDF2: i ≤ 10,000,000) and below today's defaults. |
| KDF password | `utf8("sprout/recovery-pin/v1") || 0x00 || utf8(hid) || 0x00 || utf8(pin)`; output 32 bytes |
| `mac` (server) | `HMAC-SHA256(HKDF-SHA256(RECOVERY_PIN_HMAC_KEY, salt = empty, info = "sprout/recovery-pin/v1", 32), utf8(hid) || 0x00 || utf8(uid) || 0x00 || verifier bytes)` |
| `waitEndsAt` | epoch milliseconds (number), or null in `recoveryStatus` while no wait was asked for |
| `attemptsLeft` | integer 0..10; 0 when there is no check value. Also in the answers of `setRecoveryPin` and `recoverParentDevice` |
| Errors | not signed in recently: `failed-precondition`, `details: { reason: "recent-sign-in" }`; wrong PIN: `permission-denied`, `details: { reason: "wrong-pin", attemptsLeft }`; PIN recovery closed or absent: `failed-precondition`, `details: { reason: "pin-unavailable" }`; wait not over: `failed-precondition`, `details: { reason: "wait", waitEndsAt }`; the household's parent is another account: `failed-precondition`, `details: { reason: "other-parent" }`; the device is not PENDING (already ACTIVE: the client just carries on; REVOKED: start again): `failed-precondition`, `details: { reason: "not-pending", status }`, except an ACTIVE device that this recovery admitted and whose row still carries `sessionsRevocationPending`: the call finishes the revocation and answers as the first call would have; bad proof: `permission-denied` as `verifyDeviceProof` answers |
| Rate limits | `setRecoveryPin`: ip 30/h, device 10/h; `recoveryStatus`: ip 60/h, device 30/h; `recoverParentDevice`: ip 20/h, device 10/h |

## Consequences

- A sole parent recovers in the app, on the same phone or another, with the Google account and the PIN; without the PIN after 7 days. The operator script stays for the cases the app cannot handle, and counts the same 7 days from the same request.
- **T8 is relaxed:** a second factor (the PIN, or 7 days during which a working phone can object) replaces "an existing device approves" when the parent chooses recovery. Someone with the Google account and the PIN owns the household; someone with the Google account alone owns it after 7 days unless a working phone removes theirs (the T41 residual: no push messages, so a parent who does not open Sprout for 7 days does not see it). Tamper evidence: the real phone is revoked and erases itself with the "removed from the household" notice at its next token refresh or refused write, and the recovery is recorded in `recoveries`.
- A kid with the parent's unlocked phone can still clear Sprout's storage and start the 7-day wait; the parent who opens Sprout in that week sees the sign-in screen, signs in and restores with the PIN. Clearing storage itself cannot be prevented. The same kid can also use up the ten PIN attempts, which leaves the parent the wait as well; whoever holds the phone when the wait ends then chooses the PIN. That is a dispute inside the family that the app records (`recoveries`, the removed device) and does not settle.
- A requested wait does not lapse: after the 7 days the waiting phone can take over at any later time until a working phone removes it. The working phone's card says so.
- A restore ends every session of the account, the restored phone's own included, so the parent signs in with Google twice (once to be recognised, once after the restore). When the Auth call fails, the old phones are refused for writes at once but keep reading until the revocation is finished: the server tries three times, and the restored phone's next signed call finishes it.
- A PIN check value now exists on the server (PRIVACY, DATA_SAFETY: account data, not shared, deleted with the household). Guessing is limited to 10 attempts online for as long as the PIN stays the same; offline guessing needs the server secret as well as the database.
- The parent PIN is now worth guessing remotely for someone who already holds the Google account. Six digits and ten attempts in total keep that at 1 in 100,000.

## Alternatives considered

- **The Google account alone, at once.** What most apps do, and what the kid with the parent's phone needs. Rejected for the reason in Context.
- **A recovery code shown once at set-up.** No server-side PIN value, high entropy; but one more secret to lose, and nothing for households that already exist. Could be added later as an option that replaces the PIN check.
- **A shorter or no wait without the PIN.** The wait is the only protection left when the PIN is unknown; 7 days matches the operator path so the two cannot be played against each other.
